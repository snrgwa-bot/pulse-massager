5 ways to unwind your neck and shoulders after a long day

October 6, 2026 · 4 min read · Essential Dev Team

A day at a desk, a commute spent looking down at a phone, an evening on the sofa with a laptop: they all load tension into the same small area — the back of the neck and the tops of the shoulders. None of these habits is going away, but a few minutes of deliberate unwinding at the end of the day can make the evening feel noticeably lighter. Here are five simple ways to do it.

1. Reset your posture before anything else

Stand or sit tall, let your arms hang, and slowly roll your shoulders up, back and down five times. Then gently draw your chin straight back, as if making a small double chin, and hold for a breath. It sounds too simple to matter, but it brings your head back over your shoulders, where your neck doesn't have to work so hard to hold it up.

2. Breathe slower than feels natural

Shallow, quick breathing often goes hand in hand with raised, tight shoulders. Try breathing in through your nose for a count of four and out for a count of six, for about a minute. Let your shoulders drop a little further with each exhale. Many people find that long, slow out-breaths are the quickest way to tell the body the working day is over.

3. Add gentle warmth

A warm shower aimed at the back of the neck, or a warm (never hot) towel across your shoulders for ten minutes, is an easy, comforting ritual. Warmth helps many people feel looser before stretching.

4. Stretch slowly, never bounce

Tilt one ear towards the same shoulder until you feel a mild stretch along the side of your neck, and hold for about 20 seconds while breathing slowly. Switch sides. Then clasp your hands in front of you and round your upper back, pushing your hands away. Keep every stretch comfortable: a stretch should feel like relief, not strain.

5. Finish with a few minutes of gentle vibration

This is where PULSE fits in. Open the app, choose Soft or Medium, and pick a calm rhythm from the Relax tab — Waves and Breeze are good places to start, and both are free. Hold your iPhone flat against the top of your shoulder or the muscle at the side of your neck, and let it sit there for a minute or two before moving to the other side.

  • Set the auto-off timer so you can close your eyes without watching the clock. Free sessions run for 5 minutes, which is plenty for a short evening reset.
  • Lower the speed if a rhythm feels busy. A slower beat tends to feel more soothing at the end of the day.
  • Try the Journey (PULSE Pro) when you want a hands-off session: it moves through six stages on its own and winds down at the end.

Keep the phone on soft tissue rather than directly on bone, and stop if anything feels uncomfortable. Long sessions can warm your iPhone a little, so short sessions are usually best.

Make it a habit, not a project

You don't need all five every night. Pick two — say, the shoulder rolls and five minutes of PULSE while you breathe slowly — and attach them to something you already do, like right after you close your laptop. Small and consistent beats long and occasional.
